Investment Thesis

Eternal's investment case is increasingly defined by Blinkit. The quick-commerce business now contributes the majority of group intrinsic value in my framework, which means the stock should no longer be viewed simply as a food delivery-led platform with optional upside. Instead, Eternal is effectively a Blinkit-led valuation story supported by a more stable cash-generative food delivery core. This matters because it changes the way the stock should be underwritten: future share price performance will depend far more on Blinkit's execution trajectory than on incremental improvements in Zomato's already-mature delivery business.

The key source of upside is Blinkit's ability to sustain rapid throughput growth while simultaneously improving unit economics. In practice, that means continued progress on store productivity, order density, basket expansion, contribution margins, and operating leverage as the network scales. The challenge is that these assumptions are also where the greatest valuation risk sits. If Blinkit falls short on store maturity, per-store NOV, or long-run margin delivery, the implied equity value of Eternal can compress sharply because such a large portion of the current and target valuation is concentrated in one business whose economics are still evolving.

This is why the bear case matters so much in Eternal. In my scenario analysis, the bear case DCF value falls to roughly INR 63 per share, versus INR 218 in the base case, primarily because lower Blinkit NOV assumptions, weaker terminal margins, and a higher discount rate materially reduce the value of the quick-commerce business. While that downside scenario is not my central view, it highlights how execution shortfalls in Blinkit can translate directly into substantial share price risk. In other words, the market's willingness to value Eternal at a premium is inseparable from confidence that Blinkit will deliver on long-duration growth and profitability assumptions.

Against this, Zomato's food delivery business provides an important balancing factor in the investment case. It offers a more stable earnings and cash flow base, supports strategic credibility, and reduces the probability that Eternal is viewed purely as a speculative quick-commerce vehicle. That combination of a relatively resilient core business and a high-upside but execution-sensitive growth engine is what supports my Accumulate recommendation. The stock still offers attractive upside on a blended basis, but investors must recognise that the pathway to that upside is heavily dependent on Blinkit delivering, and that is the central risk-reward trade-off in the thesis.

Company Overview

Eternal is best understood as a multi-vertical consumer internet platform built around high-frequency urban demand in India. Its four principal businesses, Zomato in food delivery, Blinkit in quick commerce, District in going-out and experiences, and Hyperpure in restaurant supply, give the company exposure to multiple consumption and merchant layers within the same ecosystem. This matters because the investment case is no longer about any single business in isolation; it is about how the platform compounds engagement, monetisation, and strategic relevance across categories.

Within that structure, Blinkit now defines the equity story. While Zomato's food delivery business remains the group's most established earnings engine, Blinkit has become the dominant driver of valuation because it sits at the centre of the market's long-duration growth expectations. As a result, Eternal should increasingly be framed not as a mature food delivery company with optional adjacencies, but as a platform where a stable core business supports an execution-sensitive quick-commerce growth asset.

The strategic logic of the company lies in the interaction among its businesses. Food delivery provides daily consumer relevance and a dense merchant network; Blinkit increases order frequency and fulfilment intensity; District expands use cases into experiences and ticketed engagement; and Hyperpure deepens merchant dependence on the platform. These interlocking businesses create a broader ecosystem narrative that is more valuable than a collection of standalone assets, even though not every segment contributes equally to near-term intrinsic value.

From a valuation standpoint, this business mix requires a sum-of-the-parts lens. Zomato should be assessed on earnings durability and cash generation, Blinkit on scale potential and future margin structure, and the smaller businesses on strategic optionality rather than near-term contribution. That is the core reason Eternal can sustain a premium narrative in the market, but it is also why the stock is highly sensitive to how investors underwrite Blinkit's execution path.

Industry Overview

The industry backdrop for Eternal is shaped by the formalisation of urban consumption in India and rising willingness to pay for convenience, speed, and digital access. Food delivery has moved into a more mature phase where scale and monetisation are translating into profitability, while quick commerce remains in its early stage of value-creation cycle and is still being valued on future potential rather than fully proven economics. This split is central to Eternal because the company operates across both a stabilising category and a still-expanding growth category.

Food delivery in India is now defined by more rational competition, stronger unit economics, and increasing monetisation through take rates and advertising. For Eternal, Zomato's position in this category provides an important earnings and cash flow anchor, which helps support valuation and offsets some of the execution risk in the rest of the portfolio. That stable core is one of the reasons the market is willing to underwrite a broader platform story.

Quick commerce remains the more strategically important and more volatile part of the industry. The category offers a large addressable market and meaningful long-term upside, but its valuation is highly sensitive to store productivity, customer density, and margin progression. This is why Eternal trades on a blended narrative of earnings support and optionality: the market is willing to assign premium multiples to Blinkit-led growth, but those multiples remain vulnerable if execution or competitive discipline weakens.

Eternal's historical financial performance reflects an exceptional increase in scale over the past five years, with reported revenue rising from INR 4,192 Cr in FY22 to INR 54,364 Cr in FY26. This headline growth captures not only category expansion in food delivery and quick commerce, but also the increasing importance of adjacencies within the group structure. Operating profit has improved materially over the same period, moving from a loss of INR 1,851 Cr in FY22 to a positive INR 1,208 Cr in FY26, which indicates that the group has moved beyond pure scale-building and is now demonstrating operating leverage in parts of the business. On the surface, this suggests a platform that is maturing financially even as it continues to invest in new growth vectors.

That said, the consolidated financials need to be interpreted with care. Eternal is no longer a single-business company, and the reported numbers aggregate segments with very different margin structures, investment needs, and valuation profiles. Zomato's food delivery business contributes far more to earnings quality than Blinkit or District at this stage, while Blinkit contributes disproportionately to group valuation because of its long-duration growth profile. As a result, historical reported performance is useful for understanding scale and operating progress, but it is not sufficient on its own to capture the economics of the stock. This is one reason a sum-of-the-parts framework is more informative than a single consolidated multiple when assessing Eternal's financial trajectory and valuation.

Quarterly trends also reinforce the picture of strong operating momentum through FY26, although the pace of growth became more uneven as the year progressed. Total adjusted revenue increased from INR 4,520 Cr in Q1 FY25 to INR 17,612 Cr in Q4 FY26, with the sharpest step-up occurring in Q2 FY26, when quarterly adjusted revenue rose 84.7% sequentially. Total B2C NOV also remained strong, increasing from INR 13,020 Cr in Q1 FY25 to INR 26,879 Cr in Q4 FY26, with QoQ growth peaking in the mid-teens before moderating to 4.5% by Q4 FY26. Taken together, these trends suggest that the business is still scaling at a high level, but they also indicate that investors should increasingly focus not just on topline expansion, but on the quality and sustainability of growth as the base becomes larger.
